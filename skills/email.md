## Formality switching — the most important rule

Michael has two distinct registers. Match the register to the recipient.

### Formal register — use for:
- Corporate professionals (recruiters, executives, EA/coordinators)
- First-contact outreach to people at companies (McKinsey, Adobe, Protiviti, etc.)
- Any email where recipient has a title (Senior EA, Principal, VP, etc.)

**Formal signals:**
- Greeting: "Hi [First name],"
- Opener: specific, personalized (see openers section below)
- Closing: "Sincerely," + full signature block
- Tone: warm but polished, no slang
- Always offer next steps explicitly ("Please let me know if you have any questions")

### Casual register — use for:
- Peers, fellow students, brothers in AKPsi
- Professionals who have already matched casual energy (e.g., responded with "Hey Michael")
- Internal coordination within student orgs

**Casual signals:**
- Greeting: "Hey [First name],"
- Opener: light and direct ("So excited to hop on a call!")
- Closing: "Michael" or just the signature block, no "Sincerely"
- Tone: friendly, relaxed, brief
- Emoji reactions (👍 🙂 💖) are fine to close low-stakes threads

## Openers — vary these, never use the same one twice in a thread

**Avoid overusing:** "I hope you're doing well!" (Michael's default — swap it out)

**Formal openers to rotate through:**
- "I hope your week is going well!"
- "I hope your day is going well!"
- Reference something specific: "Congratulations on [recent news/achievement]!"
- Reference context: "Thank you again for organizing everything — I wanted to follow up on..."
- Post-meeting: "It was great meeting you [at X / on our call]!"

**Casual openers:**
- Jump straight in: "So excited to hop on a call today!"
- "Thanks for [calling / connecting / the quick reply]!"
- Light check-in: "Hope you're having a great weekend!"

## Structural patterns

### Scheduling / calendar invites
- Always offer to send a Google Calendar invite
- Include timezone explicitly: "(GMT-07:00) Pacific Time - Los Angeles"
- Subject line format for connect calls: "[Their name] x Michael - Connect"
- Offer flexibility: list 2–3 time options before sending the invite, or ask what works

### Logistics / coordination emails
- Lead with the key info or change upfront ("We've had a small change to our list...")
- CC all relevant parties
- End with an offer to help: "Please let me know if you need any additional information"
- Keep it tight — no filler paragraphs

### Cold outreach / networking
- One sentence on who you are and the connection point (LinkedIn, referral, mutual contact)
- One sentence on why you're reaching out
- Specific ask (coffee chat, 15-min call, intro)
- Close with flexibility: "My schedule is pretty flexible — here are some times that work..."

### Thank-you emails
- Send within 24 hours of a call or event
- Reference one or two specific things discussed ("I really enjoyed learning about X" / "your stories about Y are a constant reminder of why I want to...")
- Include a forward-looking ask if appropriate ("I remember you mentioned you have friends working in X — I'd love to get connected")

### Follow-up emails
- Wait 1–2 weeks before following up unless time-sensitive
- Keep it brief: one sentence of context, one sentence of ask
- Don't apologize for following up — just be direct and friendly
- Example: "Hi [name], just following up on this thread — please let me know what I can do to help move things forward!"

## Phrases to avoid / freshen up

- ❌ "I hope you're doing well!" — overused, swap for something specific
- ❌ Vague link-only emails (just a URL + signature) — always add one line of context
- ❌ Starting every email with the same opener — vary it

## Email length guidelines

| Type | Target length |
|------|--------------|
| Cold outreach | 3–5 sentences |
| Scheduling / logistics | 2–4 sentences |
| Thank-you note | 4–7 sentences |
| Follow-up | 2–3 sentences |
| Intro / multi-party thread | 5–8 sentences |

Keep it tight. Michael's best emails are short, specific, and action-oriented.
